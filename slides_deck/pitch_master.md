# PART D — Q&A Backpocket (1:30 budget)

Anticipated questions + prepped answers. Each ≤ 30s spoken.

## Q1: "Why is MM7 (very remote) higher quality than MM1 (cities)?"

> "Two structural reasons. One — for-profit operators avoid MM6/7; remote facilities are almost entirely government or not-for-profit, which score higher across the board. Two — remote facilities are smaller, embedded in tight communities, and face less competitive pressure to cut staff costs. The pattern holds within every year of data — it's not a sample-size artifact."

## Q2: "Is the for-profit quality gap just confounded by location or case mix?"

> "We tested that. The 0.57-star gap holds **inside every remoteness band** — MM1 cities, MM2 inner regional, all the way through. In MM5 small rural towns the gap actually widens to 0.56 points. If it were location-confounded, the gap would shrink in the urban band where for-profits cluster. It doesn't."

## Q3: "How did you forecast 2025?"

> "Only population is projected — three scenarios: current ABS trend, faster aging at +4% per year, or no growth. Quality scores, supply numbers, and home care use real 2025 data from the latest GEN release. We didn't model quality or supply because they depend on policy enforcement, which is unpredictable. Population growth has 5 years of baseline data — that we can project honestly."

## Q4: "What's a 'Care Gap Index'? Did you invent it?"

> "Yes. It's our metric: residential access rate divided by quality score. Higher = more demand pressure per quality star. We chose it because it captures *both* sides of the gap simultaneously — a region with high demand but low quality scores high; a region with low demand but high quality scores low. The two single-axis metrics (access OR quality alone) hide regions that fail on both."

## Q5: "Where does the '58 crisis zones' number come from?"

> "Waitlist Pressure greater than 1.0 — meaning more high-needs home-care users than residential beds available, per SA3. We measure 2024 (latest year with both supply and demand data). 58 out of 323 analysed SA3s cross that threshold. The list is regenerable in the dashboard — filter to your state, see which of your communities are on it."

## Q6: "What if our region isn't on the top-10?"

> "Filter to your state. The dashboard's top-10 movers list is **state-aware** — pick Tasmania, you get the worst Tasmanian regions. Pick NSW, you get the worst NSW regions. The 58 zones span every state except ACT — there's no state where this isn't a problem."
