Section 3
Key Limitations & Time Filter Constraints
The most important thing to understand before interpreting any chart: not all data sources cover the same years. The overlap determines what the dashboard can actually show.
⚠️ master_sa3.csv — Only 2023 and 2024

master_sa3.csv is the pre-joined file used by Ch1, Ch2, and Ch3. It only exists for years where all three source datasets overlap: star_ratings (2023–2026), service_users (2023–2025), and abs_population (2019–2024). The intersection is 2023 and 2024 only. All care_gap_index, access_rate, waitlist_pressure, and quality_score charts on the main chapters are locked to these two years. The year radio in Tab A lets users switch between 2023 and 2024 — there is no 2025 option because service_users 2025 has not yet been joined into master_sa3.

⚠️ star_ratings_by_facility.csv — Quarterly but merged as annual

Star ratings exist for 12 quarterly snapshots (May 2023, Aug 2023, Dec 2023, Feb 2024, May 2024, Jul 2024, Nov 2024, Feb 2025, May 2025, Aug 2025, Oct 2025, Feb 2026). When merged into master_sa3.csv, ratings are averaged across all quarters within a calendar year. Ch4 (Mandate Effect) uses the raw quarterly data directly and can show the full timeline up to Feb 2026.

⚠️ abs_population — Ends at 2024 (ABS release lag)

The ABS population dataset covers 2019–2024. There is no 2025 population data yet. This means beds_per_1k and access_rate cannot be computed for 2025 even though supply (2025) and user data (2025) both exist. On the Full Map page, the "Beds per 1,000 elderly" metric maxes out at 2024 for this reason.

Supply data — 2019→2025

service_supply_by_sa3.csv has the widest coverage: 7 annual snapshots from 2019 to 2025. The Ch1 "Decline" tab and the Full Map "Number of Facilities" metric can show this full range. This is the only source that lets us track the 6-year consolidation story (−104 facilities, +12,270 beds).

Service users — Starts at 2023

service_users_by_sa3.csv only goes back to 2023 because GEN SA3-level snapshots were not published before then. This means waitlist_pressure and HCP level breakdowns cannot be tracked before 2023. We cannot say "the waitlist was smaller in 2021" — that data simply does not exist at SA3 level.

Sidebar filter scope

The State and MMM sidebar filters propagate to master_filt which feeds Ch1, Ch2, Ch3, and the Home page KPIs. They do not filter the Full Map page — the map always shows all of Australia regardless of sidebar state.

CURF / ACPR limitation

Individual-level CURF records (admissions, demographics by NESB or Indigenous status) are only available at ACPR level (73 regions), not SA3 (~330 regions). Any demographic equity analysis must be scoped as ACPR-only — it cannot be joined to SA3 or shown on the choropleth map.

Chapter 1
Ch 1 — The Map
Is aged care near me any good?
Start with geography. Let families find their suburb. Then reveal the surprise.

The headline metric is care_gap_index (access_rate ÷ quality_score). Each SA3 gets a score — high means more people in care but lower quality. The map reveals that the worst regions are not remote outposts but inner-suburban metropolitan areas, especially in WA, QLD, and VIC.

1
The crisis is in the city, not the outback

Every single SA3 in the top 10 worst care gap list is MM1 — major city. Unley (SA): CGI 2.78 National avg: 1.13 WA + QLD + VIC = 8/10 worst

2
Australia relies more on residential care than peers — but covers fewer people overall

Australia has the 3rd highest share of elderly in residential care (5.6% of 65+) yet total coverage ~8–9% sits below the OECD average of 12.7%. Sweden and Denmark reach 14–16% via home care. 47.6 beds/1,000 aged 65+

3
Supply is declining per capita — beds falling faster than population grows

Beds per 1,000 people aged 70+ fell from 76 (2020) to 67 (2025) — a 12% drop in 5 years. 103 facilities closed since 2021 vs only 85 new. Net −18 nationally

4
ACT has the highest care gap — driven by demand, not poor quality

ACT CGI: 1.374 (highest state) but quality: 3.61★ (near average). Tasmania is inverse: lowest access, highest quality (3.77★). ACT access: 5.01% TAS access: 2.92%

5
Counterintuitive: remote areas score better quality than cities

MM7 (Very Remote): 3.90★ vs MM1 (Major City): 3.55★. The remote disadvantage is access (fewer beds, longer distances), not quality. Remote facilities are mostly government-run and not-for-profit. MM7 quality: 3.90★ MM1 quality: 3.55★

6
79% of regions improved 2023→2024; 21% worsened

250 SA3s improved 66 SA3s worsened — worsening regions cluster in MM1 areas with highest for-profit concentration.

7
Home care partially compensates in remote areas — but waitlist means it barely reaches anyone

National coverage: residential ~4%, home care ~6–7%, combined ~10–11%. 88,000+ approved but not receiving home care packages nationally. 120,000+ still awaiting assessment

8
Supply consolidation: 104 fewer facilities but 12,270 more beds nationally

The market is consolidating into fewer, larger providers. Every state lost beds per 1,000 elderly (2019→2024). NT and SA: −7.4 each. 2019: 2,877 facilities 2025: 2,773 facilities

Ch 2 — The Correlation
Who runs the best facilities?
The Chapter 1 map left a crime scene. Chapter 2 names the culprit: ownership type, not location.

Government and not-for-profit facilities consistently outperform private ones across every geography and every year. The geography story from Ch1 is really an ownership story — metro areas score lower because they are more privatised.

1
The ownership gap is 0.57 stars — and has not moved in 3 years

Government avg: 4.07★. For-profit avg: 3.50★. Gap stable across all 12 quarterly snapshots May 2023–Feb 2026. Externally validated: for-profit 8× less likely to achieve 4–5 stars (Chu et al., JAMDA 2024, n=2,476 facilities). Gap: 0.57 pts

2
Staffing drives the entire gap — clinical outcomes are identical

Staffing sub-rating: Government 4.39 vs For-Profit 2.61 (gap: −1.78 pts). Quality Measures: Government 3.52 vs For-Profit 3.51 — virtually identical. For-profit deploys fewer staff hours but produces similar clinical outcomes. Staffing gap: 1.78 pts Quality Measures gap: 0.01 pts

3
The gap is not a geography artefact — it holds within every remoteness band

MM1 cities: Government 4.03 vs For-Profit 3.50 (−0.53 pts). MM5 rural: Government 4.19 vs For-Profit 3.63 (−0.56 pts, gap widens). MM6–MM7 remote: zero for-profit facilities exist. Holds across all 5 MMM bands tested

4
Metro areas are the most privatised — 43.6% of MM1 facilities are for-profit

For-profit concentration drops with remoteness: MM1 43.6% → MM2 28.7% → MM3 21.7% → MM4 18% → MM5 8.9% → MM6/MM7 0%. This is exactly why Ch1 shows quality patches in major cities.

5
For-profit receives the most public funding per facility — yet scores lowest

For-profit: $7.46M per facility. NFP: $5.54M. Government: $2.85M. For-profit receives 2.6× more public money than government facilities and returns the lowest quality score. For-profit: 2.6× more funding than government

6
The mandate lifted all types equally — the structural gap did not close

October 2023 mandate raised quality across all ownership types. Government improved; for-profit improved by a similar amount. The gap between them has not narrowed at any point in the 12 quarterly snapshots.

Ch 3 — The Reveal
Which communities are being left behind?
Numbers become people. 58 communities where elderly Australians are stuck waiting.

waitlist_pressure = hcp_high_needs ÷ residential_places. When >1.0, there are more high-needs home care users than residential beds — the system is using home care as a holding pen for people who clinically belong in facilities.

1
Home care is doing residential's job

59.1% of all home care approvals in 2025 are Level 3 or Level 4 — people requiring significant daily or near-constant clinical support. L3+L4 population grew from 140,968 (2023) to 172,285 (2025). +22% in 2 years 172,285 people

2
58 communities in structural deficit (pressure >1.0)

Worst case: Central Highlands (Tas.) at 3.111 — 56 people competing for 18 beds. National median: 0.636. 8 regions have demand more than double supply. 58 SA3s in deficit 8 SA3s at 2×+ demand

3
This is not a remote problem — 12 of the 20 worst are MM1 cities

Top 5: Central Highlands TAS (3.111), Noosa Hinterland QLD (2.833), Sunshine Coast Hinterland (2.615), Wheat Belt North WA (2.612), Gympie–Cooloola QLD (2.324). The crisis is geographic — where the beds are — not demographic.

4
The problem is supply, not demand — combined access is nearly identical

Low pressure SA3s: combined access 9.66%. High pressure SA3s: combined access 9.19%. People in crisis regions use the aged care system at the same rate — they are just stuck in the wrong tier. Residential access in worst 20 regions: 1.84% vs national 4.02%

5
Getting worse: +10 crisis zones in 12 months

2023→2024: 14 new SA3s crossed pressure = 1.0 threshold. Only 4 resolved. Net +10 in 12 months. New entrants span NT, WA, SA, VIC, TAS, QLD — all remoteness bands. +14 new crisis zones −4 resolved

6
Double crisis: some communities have both high waitlist pressure and low quality

The Hills District QLD: pressure 2.029, quality 2.97★. Kwinana WA: pressure 2.024, quality 3.19★. Hospital system cost: 438,779 bed-days lost in 2022–23 to aged care delays. 2,500 patients medically cleared but stuck in hospital.

7
Supply divergence: new beds going to the wrong places

58 crisis SA3s collectively lost 551 residential places 2019–2024. Rest of Australia added 12,027. New supply is growing nationally but systematically missing the communities with highest demand. Crisis zones: −551 places Rest of Australia: +12,027 places

Ch 4 — The Mandate
Did the October 2023 staffing mandate work?
Yes — but only for inputs, not outcomes. Staff hours rose. Resident health outcomes didn't move.

From 1 October 2023: minimum 200 minutes direct care per resident per day (40 min RN). Raised to 215 min / 44 min RN from October 2024. Background: Royal Commission found 57.6% of residents in understaffed facilities (2021).

1
Quality rose +0.25 pts nationally — a clear step-change

National avg quality: 3.40 → 3.65 (+7.3%) in the two quarters after October 2023. Visible across every state. All 8 states/territories improved in absolute terms. +0.25 pts +7.3% lift

2
More staff, same health outcomes — an uncomfortable truth

Staffing sub-rating: +0.51 pts. Compliance: +0.29 pts. Residents' Experience: +0.22 pts. Quality Measures (health outcomes): −0.015 pts — essentially flat. Confirmed externally: Harrison et al., JAMDA 2025 — "no meaningful association between care minutes and quality outcomes." Staffing: +0.51 Health outcomes: −0.015

3
Only 1 in 4 facilities actually complied at launch

26.1% fully compliant at Dec 2023, rising to 65.2% by Feb 2026. 24/7 RN requirement: 93.5% compliance (easier to measure). 11 providers / 27 facilities capped at 1 star by ACQSC in Jan 2025. Dec 2023: 26.1% compliant Feb 2026: 65.2% compliant

4
NT went from last to first; VIC got relatively left behind

NT: +0.796 pts (largest gain of any state, rose from rank 8 → rank 1). VIC: +0.311 pts (started highest, fell to rank 5 as others caught up). No state declined in absolute terms. NT: +0.796 pts

5
16 of 323 regions are still declining — the mandate hasn't reached them

303/323 SA3 regions show improving quality trend. 16/323 still declining. Worst: Esperance WA (−0.07 pts/quarter). Gold Coast Hinterland and Port Douglas–Daintree also declining. 16 SA3s still declining

6
Rural areas need 95,000 more workers just to match city staffing

Metro: 317 aged care workers per 1,000 elderly. Rural: 256 (−19%). Remote: 245 (−23%). To reach metro parity: rural needs ~95,342 additional FTE; remote needs ~12,958. The mandate cannot take hold where there are not enough workers to hire.

7
The mandate failed hardest where it was most needed — MM5 small rural towns

MM5 is 2.38× overrepresented among declining SA3s: 11.8% of MM5 SA3s declining vs 3.8% of MM1 metro SA3s. 7 of 16 declining SA3s are in QLD — a separate state-specific pattern. MM5: 2.38× overrepresented in declining regions

8
Private facilities lag 54 percentage points behind government on compliance

Government: 84.1% fully compliant (Feb 2026). For-profit: 30.2%. Same ownership divide as Ch2 quality gap. Counterintuitively: remote areas have higher compliance (MM7: 82.9%) driven by government-heavy facility mix. 54 pp gap in compliance
