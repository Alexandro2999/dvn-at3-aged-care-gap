# Australia's Aged Care Gap — Story, Pitch & Dashboard Blueprint

---

## Part 1 — The Full Story

### Narrative framing: The Detective

The dashboard opens a case file. A national map of Australian communities, coloured by how much pressure their aged care system is under. The viewer's first instinct is to look outback — remote Australia must be worst. The data immediately contradicts them. Every single community in the top 10 is a major city suburb.

That's the crime. The next three chapters find the culprit, count the victims, and evaluate whether the government's intervention is working.

---

### Chapter 1 — The Map: "The crisis is in the city, not the outback"

**Opening number:** Unley (SA) has a Care Gap Index of **2.78** — more than **2.4× the national average of 1.13**.

Every SA3 in the top 10 worst list is MM1 — major city. No rural or remote region appears.

**Counterintuitive finding (lead with this):**
Remote communities (MM7) score **3.90 stars** on quality — the highest of any band, and **+0.35 pts above major cities** (3.55). The remote disadvantage is not quality — it is scarcity. MM7 has a residential access rate of only 2.72% because there are barely any beds.

**State picture:**
ACT leads the care gap (1.374) driven by the highest demand rate in the country (5.01% of its 65+ in residential care). Tasmania is the inverse — lowest gap, highest quality (3.769 stars), least demand pressure.

**Supply collapse (new finding):**
Between 2019 and 2025, Australia lost **104 residential facilities** nationally — but added **12,270 beds**. The market is consolidating: smaller facilities close, survivors get larger. Yet every state still lost beds per 1,000 elderly people:

| State | Change (beds/1,000 aged 65+, 2019→2024) |
|-------|-----------------------------------------|
| NT | −7.4 |
| SA | −7.4 |
| NSW | −5.4 |
| QLD | −5.4 |
| VIC | −4.7 |

**119 SA3s lost at least one facility; only 79 gained.** Even WA and ACT — the only states that gained facilities — still saw beds per capita fall because population growth outpaced new supply.

---

### Chapter 2 — The Correlation: "It's not where your facility is. It's who owns it."

**The culprit:** ownership type.

Government: **4.07 stars** | Not-for-Profit: **3.61 stars** | For-Profit: **3.50 stars**

The 0.57-point gap between government and for-profit is **stable across every quarter** from May 2023 to February 2026. The October 2023 mandate lifted all ownership types — but did not compress the gap between them.

**The gap is entirely a staffing gap, not a clinical outcomes gap:**

| Sub-rating | Government | For-Profit | Gap |
|-----------|-----------|------------|-----|
| **Staffing** | 4.39 | 2.61 | **−1.78 pts** |
| Quality Measures (health outcomes) | 3.52 | 3.51 | **≈ 0** |

For-profit facilities are not producing worse clinical outcomes — they are deploying fewer staff hours to achieve the same result. Whether that is efficiency or accumulated risk, the data cannot say.

**The geography is not the confounder:**
Within MM1 major cities: Government 4.03 vs For-Profit 3.50 (−0.53 pts). The gap holds inside every remoteness band and widens in rural areas.

**The privatisation map:**
For-profit owns **43.6% of major city facilities** — the highest concentration of any band. In MM6–MM7 (remote/very remote): **0%**. This is why the Chapter 1 map shows patches of low quality in major cities: it is a concentration-of-for-profit problem.

**The efficiency paradox:**
For-profit receives **$7.46M per facility** in government funding — **2.6× more** than government-run facilities ($2.85M) — yet scores lowest on quality.

**External validation:** Chu et al. JAMDA 2024 (2,476 facilities): for-profit facilities are **8× less likely** to achieve 4–5 stars than government-run (OR 0.12).

---

### Chapter 3 — The Reveal: "The wait is real, growing, and the beds are going to the wrong places"

**The scale:**
- **58 SA3 regions** have more high-needs home care users than available residential beds (waitlist pressure > 1.0) — nearly 1 in 6 nationally
- **172,285 people** at Level 3 or 4 in home care — people who clinical assessors say need residential beds (+22% in 2 years)
- **59.1%** of all home care approvals in 2025 are L3 or L4

Home care was built to support light daily needs at home. It has become a holding pen for people who need residential care but cannot access a bed.

**This is not a remote problem:**
12 of the 20 worst-pressure regions are **MM1 major cities**. The crisis is geographic — about where beds are — not demographic.

**Worst communities (2024):**

| SA3 | State | High-needs people | Beds | Pressure |
|-----|-------|------------------|------|---------|
| Central Highlands | TAS | 56 | 18 | 3.11 |
| Noosa Hinterland | QLD | 255 | 90 | 2.83 |
| Sunshine Coast Hinterland | QLD | 659 | 252 | 2.62 |
| Wheat Belt – North | WA | 964 | 369 | 2.61 |

**Getting worse fast:**
In a single year (2023→2024): **14 new communities** crossed the pressure = 1.0 threshold. Only **4 resolved** their deficit. Net: **+10 crisis zones in 12 months**.

**New finding — beds being built in the wrong places:**
The 58 crisis zones collectively **lost 551 residential places** (2019→2024) while the rest of Australia added **12,027**. Supply is growing nationally — but flowing to communities that already have enough.

**System-wide cost:**
**438,779 hospital bed-days** consumed in 2022–23 by patients waiting for aged care placement. **10% of hospital beds** currently blocked by patients who cannot be discharged because aged care is unavailable.

---

### Chapter 4 — The Mandate Effect: "Better on paper. Not yet in practice."

The October 2023 staffing mandate required every residential facility to provide a minimum 200 minutes of direct care per resident per day (40 minutes from a registered nurse).

**What worked:**
National average quality rose from **3.40 → 3.65 (+0.25 pts)** — a clear step-change visible in every state within two quarters. All 8 states improved. NT went from last to first (+0.796 pts).

**What didn't:**
Quality Measures — the sub-rating measuring **actual resident health outcomes** — moved **−0.015 pts**. The mandate improved staffing inputs. Outcomes have not followed.

Full compliance was **26.1% at December 2023**, rising to **65.2% by February 2026**.

**Who is still failing:**
For-profit compliance: **30.2%** fully compliant vs Government: **84.1%** — a **54 percentage-point gap**. The same ownership divide that drives the quality gap also drives the compliance gap.

**Geographic failure:**
MM5 small rural towns are **2.38× overrepresented** among the 16 still-declining SA3 regions. Rural areas have 256 aged care workers per 1,000 elderly — **19% fewer than metro** (317). The mandate cannot take hold where there are not enough workers to hire.

---

### The through-line (one paragraph)

Australia's aged care system is under the most pressure in its most populated cities — not the outback. The root cause is ownership: for-profit facilities dominate metro markets, receive more public funding per site, and deliver consistently lower quality. The consequence is a growing cohort of high-needs elderly people stuck in home care — a system built for light support — while new beds are being built in communities that already have enough. The government's 2023 staffing mandate improved inputs on paper. It has not yet reached the people and places that need it most.

## Part 2 — How to Present (Pitch Guide)

### Recommended stakeholder hat

**"CEO of a mid-size residential aged care provider considering where to build next."**

Why this works:
- Lets you present all three audience lenses through one persona (families = your customers; workers = your pipeline; market gaps = your investment case)
- Gives you permission to engage with both the human story AND the numbers without switching tone
- Rubric asks to "prove a point and drive action" — the action is clear: *build in the right places*

Alternative: **"State Health Minister of Queensland"** — stronger emotional pull (7 of 16 declining SA3s are in QLD), but constrains the business/investor angle.

---

### 5-minute structure

| Time | Moment | What to show |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **0:00–0:30** | Open with the map | Choropleth of care gap index. Ask: "Which communities do you think are worst?" Let them guess outback. Reveal: every top 10 is a major city. |
| **0:30–1:30** | Name the culprit | Switch to Ch2 ownership bar chart. "It's not location — it's who owns the facility. For-profit homes receive 2.6× more funding and return the lowest quality." |
| **1:30–2:30** | Count the victims | Ch3 waitlist pressure bar. "58 communities. 172,285 people clinically assessed as needing a bed — stuck in home care. In Central Highlands Tasmania: 56 people, 18 beds." |
| **2:30–3:30** | Show the supply failure | Ch3 supply divergence line. "New beds are going to the wrong places. Crisis zones lost 551 beds while the rest of Australia added 12,027." |
| **3:30–4:30** | Policy check | Ch4 mandate quality timeline. "The government acted in October 2023. Quality rose — but only on paper. Resident health outcomes: flat." |
| **4:30–5:00** | Call to action | Return to the map. "This dashboard is not a report. It is a tool. Filter to your state, your suburb, your parent's postcode. Then make a decision with the actual numbers." |

---

### Q&A preparation

| Likely question | Answer |
|----------------|--------|
| "Isn't remote always worse?" | No — MM7 scores 3.90 stars, the highest quality of any band. The remote problem is access (distance, scarcity), not quality. |
| "Are you saying for-profit is bad?" | We're saying ownership type is associated with lower quality inside every geography band tested. The gap is 0.57 stars and has not moved in 3 years. That warrants the question. |
| "Has the mandate worked?" | Staffing improved (+0.51 pts on the staffing sub-rating). Resident health outcomes moved −0.015 pts. The jury is still out on whether improved inputs translate to better lives. |
| "Why aren't new beds going to crisis zones?" | The data doesn't tell us why — only that it's happening. The 58 crisis zones lost 551 beds while the rest of Australia added 12,027. That is a market failure in geographic allocation. |

### 3 Advanced features to implement

#### Feature 1 — Context-aware filtering (narrative text updates)
When user selects a state in the sidebar, the callout text at the top of each tab updates to reflect that state's specific numbers.

```python
if len(selected_states) == 1:
    state = selected_states[0]
    st.info(f"{state}: Care Gap Index {state_gap:.2f} | Quality {state_quality:.2f} stars | "
            f"Beds per 1,000 elderly: {beds:.1f}")
else:
    st.info("National average: Care Gap 1.13 | Quality 3.65 stars")
```

#### Feature 2 — What-if parameterisation (Tab 1 / Tab 3)
Slider: "Minimum beds per 1,000 elderly" — recolours choropleth to highlight SA3s below threshold.

```python
threshold = st.slider("Flag SA3s with fewer than X beds per 1,000 elderly aged 65+",
                      min_value=10, max_value=80, value=40, step=5)
# SA3s below threshold coloured red regardless of care_gap_index
```

A second slider in Tab 3: "Crisis threshold" — adjust the pressure = 1.0 line to explore sensitivity.

#### Feature 3 — Narrative scrollytelling (Tab 3)
Use `st.container()` sections that reveal progressively as the user scrolls through the waitlist story:
- Section 1: Scale of the problem (stacked area)
- Section 2: Which communities (horizontal bar)
- Section 3: Who is waiting (stacked HCP bar)
- Section 4: Supply failure (divergence line)
- Section 5: Getting worse (threshold crossings)

Each section has a `st.info/warning/error` callout that summarises the "so what" in ≤ 2 sentences before the chart appears.
