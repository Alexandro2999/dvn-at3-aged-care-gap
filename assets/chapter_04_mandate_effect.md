# Chapter 4 — The Mandate Effect

**Question:** Has the government's staffing mandate actually improved care?
**Answer:** Facilities are hiring more staff. But residents aren't living better for it yet.

---

## Background: Why was a mandate needed?

- In 2021, the Royal Commission into Aged Care found **57.6% of residents** were living in facilities with unacceptable staffing levels `[external — Royal Commission into Aged Care Quality and Safety, 2021]`
- Before the mandate, providers set their own staffing levels — no mandatory minimum floor existed
- Commission finding: *"Providers are free to judge for themselves what staffing numbers are 'adequate'. The status quo is unacceptable."* `[external — Royal Commission into Aged Care Quality and Safety, 2021]`
- Deloitte projected a **70% workforce increase** (186k → 316k FTE) needed from 2020–2050 just to maintain the current staffing ratio `[external — Deloitte Access Economics, cited in Royal Commission Final Report, 2021]`

---

## What the mandate requires

| Date | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| 1 Jul 2023 | Registered nurse on-site **24/7** at every residential facility |
| **1 Oct 2023** | Minimum **200 minutes** direct care per resident per day, of which at least **40 minutes** must be a registered nurse |
| 1 Oct 2024 | Raised to **215 minutes** total / **44 minutes** RN |

---

## Insight 1 — Your facility is probably better than it was 2 years ago

- National average quality score: **3.40 → 3.65** (+0.25 pts) after Oct 2023 `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, May 2023–Feb 2026]`
- The increase is a clear step-change — not a slow trend; visible across every state within two quarters
- All 8 states and territories improved in absolute terms `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, May 2023–Feb 2026]`

> **Dashboard callout (success):** National average quality rose from 3.40 to 3.65 (+0.25 pts) in the two quarters following the October 2023 mandate — a step-change visible in every state.

---

## Insight 2 — More staff, same health outcomes — the numbers tell an uncomfortable truth

| Sub-rating | Before | After | Change |
|-----------|--------|-------|--------|
| Staffing | 2.49 | 3.00 | **+0.51 pts** |
| Compliance | 4.28 | 4.57 | +0.29 pts |
| Residents experience | 3.28 | 3.50 | +0.22 pts |
| **Quality measures** | 3.55 | 3.54 | **−0.015 pts** |

`[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, pre/post Oct 2023]`

- Staffing recorded the largest gain — consistent with the mandate's design
- Quality measures (resident health outcomes) were essentially flat
- Independent research confirms: *"Despite rising staffing levels, no meaningful association emerged between increased care minutes and improved resident quality measures."* `[external — Harrison et al., JAMDA 2025]`
- The mandate improved inputs. Outcomes have not yet followed.

> **Dashboard callout (warning):** Quality Measures — the sub-rating measuring actual resident health outcomes — moved just −0.015 pts. The mandate improved staffing on paper. It has not yet moved the needle on resident health.

---

## Insight 3 — Only 1 in 4 facilities actually met the new rules at launch

- Before the mandate, only an estimated **3.8%** of facilities were positioned to meet the new standards `[external — The Conversation, 2023]`
- From project data: **26.1%** fully compliant at Dec 2023 `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, Dec 2023]`, consistent with Productivity Commission's 34% for full FY 2023–24 `[external — PC RoGS 2026]`
- 24/7 RN requirement reached **93.5%** compliance — easier to measure and enforce than care minutes `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, post-mandate snapshots]`
- January 2025: ACQSC enforced against **11 providers / 27 facilities** — capped at 1 star, banned from reaching 5 stars for 3 years `[external — ACQSC, Jan 2025]`
- Star ratings reward improvement effort, not only full compliance — which explains why the staffing sub-rating rose +0.51 pts despite only 1 in 3 facilities being fully compliant at mandate launch `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv]`

> **Dashboard callout (info):** 24/7 RN compliance reached 93.5%. Full care-minutes compliance reached only 26.1% at Dec 2023 — rising to 65.2% by Feb 2026 as providers adapted to the higher bar.

## Insight 4 — NT went from last to first — VIC got left behind as other states caught up

| State | Change | Rank: first → latest |
|-------|--------|----------------------|
| NT | **+0.796 pts** | 8 → **1** |
| VIC | +0.311 pts | **1 → 5** |
| ACT | +0.289 pts | smallest gain |

`[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, May 2023 vs Feb 2026]`

- NT started from the lowest base and improved the most — the mandate had the greatest impact where the gap was largest
- VIC did not worsen in absolute terms — other states simply caught up faster
- No state declined in absolute terms `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, May 2023 vs Feb 2026]`

> **Dashboard callout (info):** NT improved +0.796 pts — the largest gain of any state, rising from last to first. No state went backwards in absolute terms.

## Insight 5 — 16 regions are still going backwards — is yours one of them?

- **303 / 323 SA3 regions** show an improving quality trend `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, quality_trend_slope across all snapshots]`
- **16 / 323 SA3 regions** are still declining — the mandate has not reached them
- Worst: **Esperance (WA)** — −0.07 pts/quarter `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, linear regression slope]`
- Gold Coast Hinterland and Port Douglas–Daintree (QLD) are also in the declining group `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv]`

> **Dashboard callout (warning):** 16 SA3 regions are still declining in quality despite the mandate. Esperance (WA) is falling at −0.07 pts per quarter — the steepest decline nationally.

---

## Insight 6 — Rural areas need 95,000 more workers just to match city staffing levels

- Metro: **317 aged care workers** per 1,000 people aged 65+ `[external — Morris et al., IJERPH, Apr 2025]`
- Rural: **256** per 1,000 `[external — Morris et al., IJERPH, Apr 2025]`
- Remote: **245** per 1,000 `[external — Morris et al., IJERPH, Apr 2025]`
- To reach metro parity: rural areas need ~**95,342 additional FTE**; remote areas need ~**12,958 additional FTE** `[external — Morris et al., IJERPH, Apr 2025]`

> **Dashboard callout (warning):** Rural aged care operates with 256 workers per 1,000 elderly — 19% fewer than metro (317). The mandate cannot take hold where there are not enough workers to hire.

---

## Insight 7 — The mandate failed hardest in the places that needed it most

- **MM5 small rural towns are 2.38× overrepresented** among declining SA3s — 11.8% (4/34) of MM5 SA3s are declining vs only 3.8% of metro SA3s `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, quality_trend_slope]`
- These regions operate with **256 aged care workers per 1,000 elderly** — 19% fewer than metro (317). The mandate cannot take hold where there are not enough workers to hire. `[external — Morris et al., IJERPH, Apr 2025]`
- **7 of 16 declining SA3s (44%)** are in rural/remote workforce tiers (MM3+), which represent only 34% of all SA3s `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv]`
- **Queensland is a separate story:** 7 of 16 declining SA3s are in QLD (44%), 5 of them in metro areas — not a workforce gap problem, a state-specific concentration issue requiring its own investigation `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv]`

> **Dashboard callout (warning):** MM5 small rural towns are 2.38× overrepresented among declining SA3s. The mandate is struggling hardest in the regions with the fewest workers to comply with it.

---

## Insight 8 — Private facilities lag 54 percentage points behind government on compliance

- Fully compliant facilities: **26.1% (Dec 2023) → 65.2% (Feb 2026)** — improving steadily over 2 years `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, Dec 2023 and Feb 2026]`
- When the target was raised in Oct 2024 (200→215 min / 40→44 RN), compliance dipped before recovering — providers are actively adapting `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, Oct 2024–Feb 2026]`
- **For-profit facilities are the least compliant** ownership type: government **84.1%** fully compliant vs for-profit **30.2%** — a 54 percentage-point gap `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, Feb 2026]`
- **Counter-intuitively, compliance is higher in remote areas** (MM7: 82.9%, MM6: 68.1%, MM5: 57.1%) than metro (MM1: 43.3%) — driven by the higher share of government-run facilities in remote regions `[data — star_ratings_by_facility.csv, Feb 2026]`

> **Dashboard callout (warning):** For-profit facilities: 30.2% fully compliant. Government facilities: 84.1%. A 54 percentage-point gap — the same ownership divide that drives the quality gap in Chapter 2 also drives the compliance gap here.
